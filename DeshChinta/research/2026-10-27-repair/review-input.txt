## Script 3 — সিপিবি ঘটনা: অভিযোগ থেকে তদন্ত

### Positioning

**Main title:** সিপিবি কার্যালয়ের ঘটনায় কোন প্রমাণ এখনও বাকি?

- হামলার অভিযোগ, পুলিশের অস্বীকার: তদন্ত কী বলছে?
- রাজনৈতিক পরিচয় নয়, প্রমাণ দিয়ে বিচার

**Thesis:** Follow the evidence and official response after the October 26 confrontation. This is an accountability follow-up, not a delayed breaking-news accusation.

**Thumbnail:** “দুই বক্তব্য / প্রমাণ কোথায়?” over source cards, not faces branded as attackers.

**Counterargument:** police disputed an attack and injuries in the early TBS account; fairly report that denial and do not infer an organised partisan assault solely from activists' statements.[86]

**Dhaka, 26 October 2026:** Transparency International Bangladesh (TIB) has strongly condemned and expressed deep concern over the Inspector General of Police’s (IGP) remarks that the police would resort to crossfire, if necessary, to maintain law and order, provided the government gives its approval. Describing the remarks as an expression of utter disregard for the Constitution and the rule of law, TIB has demanded their immediate withdrawal, accountability of the IGP, and accountability for making a declaration that contradicts the government’s stated position against extrajudicial killings.

TIB Executive Director Dr. Iftekharuzzaman said, “Is the police chief seeking to use the discredited practice of crossfire as a shield for the police’s failure to maintain law and order? Does seeking to legitimise extrajudicial killings by making them conditional on government approval amount to an admission of failure to combat crime through lawful means? The responsibility of the police is to conduct professional investigations, identify offenders and bring the accused to trial on the basis of admissible evidence. Presenting crossfire as an alternative cannot resolve institutional weaknesses; rather, it is an example of professional bankruptcy. In the same remarks, the IGP spoke of discharging duties in accordance with the law and the Constitution, while also saying that the police would resort to crossfire if the government approved it. These two positions are contradictory. No government approval can legitimise an extrajudicial killing. The rights to legal protection and to life and personal liberty guaranteed under Articles 31 and 32 of the Constitution cannot be set aside by executive decisions. Regardless of the seriousness of an allegation, the police have no authority to kill anyone by bypassing due process.”

Recalling past experiences, the TIB Executive Director said, “The conviction of police officers in the Sinha murder case, along with allegations reported in connection with the verdict that crossfire was used to extort money, torture people and kill them, serves as a reminder of the horrific nature of this practice. The killing of Teknaf municipal councillor Ekramul Haque in a purported gunfight also raised serious questions, particularly following the release of an audio recording by his family and allegations of murder. The convictions of former RAB officials in the Narayanganj seven-murder case provide another judicially established example of the criminal abuse of power by law enforcement agencies. Despite thousands of such experiences, and amid public expectations for police reform and broader state reform, the police chief is once again raising the prospect of crossfire. He must answer the question of what lessons the police have learned from past crimes.”

Dr. Zaman further said, “Such reckless and ill-considered remarks by the police chief could be perceived as an implicit licence for unlawful use of force at the field level. When the person responsible for ensuring that the force operates within the law presents crossfire as an alternative, questions about his sense of responsibility and suitability for leadership inevitably arise. How can the public trust that, under his leadership, citizens’ lives and legal protections will be safeguarded? On the other hand, on 14 September, the Law Minister stated that the government would not resort to crossfire to combat terrorism and that existing laws were sufficient to tackle terrorism. Why, then, is the police chief raising the prospect of crossfire subject to government approval? The government must clearly explain this inconsistency and specify how accountability will be ensured for implementing its declared position.”

TIB said that any use of force by the police must be lawful, necessary and proportionate. Under international standards, the intentional lethal use of firearms is permissible only when strictly unavoidable to protect life. Salahuddin Ahmed says government firmly opposes extrajudicial killings, enforced disappearances and custodial deaths

 Published : 27 October 2026

 Updated : 27 October 2026, 19:08

Home Minister Salahuddin Ahmed today told The Daily Star that the police chief’s remarks about conducting “crossfire” with government permission did not represent the government’s position.

The government was firmly opposed to extrajudicial killings, enforced disappearances and custodial deaths, he said.

Ahmed said the July mass uprising, which toppled the Awami League government in August 2024, had taken place to establish the rule of law. Upholding this principle is the government’s commitment to the people, he added.

Yesterday, Inspector General of Police Md Ali Hossain Fakir said at a programme in Dhaka’s Chawkbazar, “Wherever crossfire is necessary, if the government allows it, we will do that.”

Police Headquarters subsequently clarified that the IGP had not intended to endorse extrajudicial killings or punishment outside the judicial process. It said he was referring to firm action against criminals within the law.

The Bangladesh Police Service Association also issued a statement, saying the IGP’s comments should be understood in the context of existing laws and regulations.

The association described his remarks as a timely call for firm and uncompromising enforcement of the law. It said officers should be able to exercise their lawful powers, including the right of private defence, without unnecessary hesitation.

The association said police personnel had faced 176 attacks so far this year, leaving four officers dead and 370 seriously injured.

The statement did not address the IGP’s reference to government permission.

Under Articles 31 and 32 of the Constitution, no person can be deprived of life except in accordance with law. The government has no authority to permit extrajudicial killings in the name of “crossfire”.

Bangladesh has a long history of deaths described by law enforcement agencies as “crossfire” or “gunfights”.

The practice became widespread after the BNP-led government established the Rapid Action Battalion (Rab) in 2004. Such killings continued under the military-backed caretaker administration in 2007-08 and the Awami League government from 2009 to 2024.

According to Ain o Salish Kendra (ASK), 2,957 people were killed in alleged crossfire or gunfights involving law enforcement agencies between 2004 and September 2026.

The BNP’s 31-point state reform programme pledges to end enforced disappearances and extrajudicial killings and bring those responsible for killings in the name of crossfire to justice.

Bangladesh is scheduled to graduate from least developed country (LDC) status on 24 November 2026, following a five-year preparatory period approved by the General Assembly. This milestone reflects decades of sustained development progress across multiple indicators: income growth, improved human development outcomes and reduced structural vulnerability. The country has consistently exceeded all three graduation criteria â gross national income (GNI) per capita, the human assets index, and the economic and environmental vulnerability index â in consecutive triennial reviews, with the most recent 2024 assessment confirming eligibility, with significant margins above required thresholds.

The graduation of Bangladesh carries exceptional significance. As the largest LDC by population (15 per cent of total LDC population), gross domestic product (GDP) (26 percent of combined LDC GDP), and merchandise exports (20 per cent of total LDC exports), the countryâs transition will be closely observed as a test case for the credibility and effectiveness of the United Nations graduation framework. This significance is further amplified by Bangladeshâs extraordinary reliance on LDC-specific trade preferences and policy flexibilities: approximately three-quarters of merchandise exports enjoy duty-free market access under various preferential schemes, and the country has leveraged World Trade Organization (WTO) policy flexibilities to build the worldâs second-largest apparel export industry. No other graduating LDC has faced comparable dependence on international support measures, rendering Bangladeshâs transition uniquely complex and consequential.

He said bilateral trade showed promise, but significant opportunities remained to diversify trade and expand investment.
He noted that during Prime Minister Tarique Rahman's visit to Malaysia in June, discussions were held with Malaysian Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim on concluding a sustainable and mutually beneficial FTA by 2027.
The first round of formal negotiations marks the beginning of the institutional process to fulfil that commitment, he added.
The commerce secretary further said an FTA would be crucial for Bangladesh to address the challenges of graduating from the least developed country (LDC) category and maintaining its competitiveness in international markets.
Positioned at the crossroads of South and Southeast Asia, Bangladesh offers a promising market for Malaysian investors, he said, highlighting opportunities for joint investment in halal industries, semiconductor skills development, digital trade, logistics and special economic zones.
He also expressed gratitude for Malaysia's support for Bangladesh's aspiration to join the Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (RCEP), a regional economic bloc.
Malaysia's chief negotiator Dr Rashidi Said said the proposed FTA was not merely an agreement to reduce tariffs but a strategic initiative to establish a long-term and balanced economic partnership between the two countries.
According to him, bilateral trade between Bangladesh and Malaysia amounted to approximately $2.84 billion in 2025.
Once implemented, the agreement would create new opportunities for trade expansion, industrialisation and investment, he added.
He expressed optimism that the negotiations could be concluded by 2027 through mutual understanding, flexibility and a pragmatic action plan.
He also hoped that the Malaysian prime minister's upcoming visit to Bangladesh would give further momentum to the process.
Bangladesh's chief negotiator Khadija Nazneen said the FTA would facilitate market access in both countries, reduce trade barriers and create new opportunities for investment and employment.
It would also make trade operations more transparent, streamlined and predictable, she added.
She said the negotiations would focus on reaching a consensus on key issues, including trade in goods and services, rules of origin and customs procedures, while safeguarding the interests of both countries.
Representatives from both sides reaffirmed their commitment to reaching a balanced, effective and long-term agreement through constructive dialogue, mutual respect and flexibility.

Malaysia, Bangladesh Set To Start FTA Talks In Dhaka
27/10/2026 12:00 AM
By Shakir Husain
HYDERABAD, Oct 26 (Bernama) -- Malaysia and Bangladesh are set to start the first round of their free trade talks in Dhaka on Tuesday.
A 30-member Malaysian delegation is in the Bangladeshi capital for the three-day negotiations, Bangladesh media reported.
Bangladesh is keen to boost economic relations with its major trade partners as it prepares to graduate from the United Nations Least Developed Country (LDC) status next month.
“A free trade agreement (FTA) with Malaysia is of huge importance for Bangladesh's export competitiveness as after its graduation from the LDC status, preferential international market access will get affected,” Bangladesh-Malaysia Chamber of Commerce and Industry (BMCCI) president Mohammad Anwar Shahid told Bernama on Monday.
The two countries aim to conclude an FTA in 2027.
Bangladesh is Malaysia's second-largest trading partner in South Asia, with total trade in 2025 valued at RM12.18 billion.
Malaysia exported goods worth about RM10 billion to Bangladesh last year, with petroleum products constituting the principal export, while it imported Bangladeshi goods valued at RM2.1 billion, including textiles, apparel and footwear.

# First round of Bangladesh-Malaysia FTA talks Oct 27

 30-member Malaysian delegation arrives in Dhaka for negotiations, says commerce ministry 

 Published : 25 October 2026 

 Updated : 25 October 2026, 23:29 

The first round of negotiations for a free trade agreement (FTA) between Bangladesh and Malaysia is set to begin in Dhaka on October 27, as the Malaysian delegation has already arrived in the capital, a commerce ministry official said today.

A 30-member Malaysian delegation has arrived in Dhaka for the talks, which will be held at a city hotel, said Kamal Hossain, public relations officer of the commerce ministry.

Bangladesh has been negotiating with eight to 10 countries, including Malaysia, to secure trade deals and retain preferential market access after its scheduled graduation from the least developed country (LDC) category on November 24 this year, if the transition is not deferred.

The country has already signed an Economic Partnership Agreement (EPA) with Japan in February and a Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement (CEPA) with South Korea to secure duty-free market access after graduating from the LDC category.

However, neither the EPA nor the CEPA has come into force yet, as validation is needed by the parliaments of the respective countries.
