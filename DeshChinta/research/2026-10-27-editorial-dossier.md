## Topic 1

### TOPIC
ক্রসফায়ার বিতর্ক: সরকারের অনুমতি কি আইনের বিকল্প?

### WHAT HAPPENED
The Daily Star reports that IGP Md Ali Hossain Fakir referred on October 26 to using crossfire if the government permitted it; on October 27 Home Minister Salahuddin Ahmed told the newspaper that the remarks did not represent the government's position.[31]
TIB issued its own October 26 statement condemning the remarks and demanding withdrawal and accountability, including removal from office until accountability is ensured.[8]

### WHAT IS CONFIRMED
- TIB's statement exists on TIB's own website; its criticism and demands are confirmed as that organisation's position, not a judicial finding.[8]
- The official legal database's Article 31 protects treatment in accordance with law and guards against detrimental action to life, liberty, body, reputation or property except in accordance with law.[50]
- Article 32 states that no person shall be deprived of life or personal liberty save in accordance with law.[51]
- The Daily Star directly attributes the government's opposition to extrajudicial killings, disappearances and custodial deaths to the home minister.[31]

### WHAT IS ALLEGED
TIB argues that the IGP's language could be understood as an implicit licence for unlawful force and as a response to institutional failure; this is TIB's assessment of risk, not proof that an unlawful order was issued or acted upon.[8]
No new killing, operational instruction or individual criminal liability is established by this dossier.

### WHAT IS DISPUTED
Police Headquarters, as reported by The Daily Star, says the remarks did not endorse extrajudicial punishment and referred to lawful firm action; the Police Service Association also defended the comments in the context of lawful powers.[31]
TIB regards the reference to government permission as incompatible with constitutional safeguards and rejects executive authorisation as a justification for extrajudicial killing.[8]
These positions dispute meaning and implications. They do not establish two equally valid legal permissions to bypass due process.

### WHAT REMAINS UNKNOWN
The complete unedited speech; the original dated Police Headquarters clarification and any revised version; any subsequent apology, disciplinary decision or operational circular; whether field instructions changed. None was authenticated in this run. A reported clarification is not evidence of operational compliance.

### WHY IT MATTERS
Editorial assessment: an ordinary person's protection should not depend on whether an executive official approves force. A video can explain how to distinguish a public statement, an operational order, lawful protection of life and punishment without trial, without presuming that any new killing occurred.

### LEGAL / POLICY CONTEXT
Read Articles 31 and 32 directly rather than relying only on an advocacy organisation's summary.[50][51]
TIB states that force must be lawful, necessary and proportionate, and describes intentional lethal force as permissible only when strictly unavoidable to protect life.[8]
That international-standard summary remains attributed to TIB here. A Bangladesh-qualified reviewer should verify the governing domestic force provisions and current amendments before recording detailed legal advice. This note is not case-specific legal advice.

### HISTORICAL CONTEXT
The Daily Star describes the spread of the crossfire practice after RAB's establishment in 2004 and its continuation across the subsequent caretaker and Awami League administrations.[31]
Use this only as attributed historical reporting. Do not reproduce aggregate death totals or claim judicial findings in the Sinha, Ekramul or Narayanganj cases from this dossier: original judgments and underlying incident datasets were not reviewed.

### PRIMARY SOURCES
- TIB, October 26 press release: direct evidence of TIB's position and demands, not an independent recording of the IGP's speech.[8]
- Bangladesh legal database, Constitution Articles 31 and 32: primary legal text.[50][51]
- Still required: full speech, Police Headquarters statement versions, any administrative response. Do not label newspaper paraphrases of these documents as retrieved originals.

### RELIABLE SECONDARY SOURCES
The Daily Star, October 27, home minister interview/report; includes the police clarification and the association's defence.[31]
The minister's response is evidence of what he told the newspaper; it does not independently measure the government's conduct.

### QUESTIONS THAT REMAIN
1. Will the police release the full recording and all clarification versions?
2. What written instruction governs officers' use of force after these remarks?
3. Has the IGP withdrawn the language, or has a formal accountability process begun?
4. What independent complaint and investigation route exists for a disputed use of lethal force?
5. What records would let the public test whether the government's stated policy is followed?
