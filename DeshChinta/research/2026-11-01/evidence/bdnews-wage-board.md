# Bangladesh forms minimum wage board amid labour unrest

The government has announced the formation of a minimum wage board amid labour unrest in the readymade garment sector over demands for higher wages.

The government has also formed a separate six-member wage board to determine the minimum wage for workers in the garment industry.

In accordance with the Labour Act, the six-member board comprises the four members of the minimum wage board, along with one representative each from the garment owners and workers.

ABM Shamsuddin, a director of the Bangladesh Garment Manufacturers and Exporters Association (BGMEA), will represent the owners, while Babul Akhtar, general secretary of the Bangladesh Garments and Industrial Workers Federation, will represent the workers.

The notification requested minimum wage board chairman Ashfaqul to take the next steps towards determining the minimum wage in consultation with representatives of the owners, workers and the independent member.

Labour unrest has been ongoing in the capital for the past several days, with garment workers demanding a minimum monthly wage of Tk 30,000. On Sunday, clashes broke out between police and garment workers during a blockade in Dhaka's Mirpur area.

The last minimum wage for garment workers was announced in November 2023 and came into effect the following month. Accordingly, the current minimum wage rate for the sector is set to expire next month.

In 2023, the minimum wage for garment workers was raised from Tk 8,000 to Tk 12,500, marking an increase of 56.25 percent.
