# Bangladesh gets 3 more years before LDC graduation
## The extension gives Bangladesh until 24 November 2029 to strengthen export competitiveness, implement economic reforms and prepare for the loss of trade preferences and other LDC-specific benefits.
**The United Nations General Assembly (UNGA) has approved Bangladesh's request for a three-year extension of its preparatory period for graduation from the least developed country (LDC) category, moving the deadline from 24 November 2026.**
The decision, adopted yesterday, followed a proposal introduced by Uruguay on behalf of Bangladesh. The extension will give the country additional time to address structural weaknesses, strengthen export competitiveness and prepare for changes in international trade preferences.

...

The resolution said the assembly decides, on an exceptional basis, to extend by an additional period of three years, until 24 November 2029, the preparatory period for Bangladesh before graduation from the least developed country category occurs.

Mahmud Hasan Khan, president of the Bangladesh Garment Manufacturers and Exporters Association (BGMEA), told TBS that they had long advocated additional preparation time.
He described the UN decision as good news for Bangladesh's export sector, adding that the country must use the extended period to improve infrastructure and logistics and pursue free trade agreements (FTAs) with countries where such arrangements are necessary.
Fahmida Khatun, distinguished fellow at CPD, said simply extending the transition period will not make the economy more competitive. "Effective reforms are needed in trade, revenue collection, banking and infrastructure," she told TBS.

...

The government formally requested the extension from the United Nations Committee for Development Policy (UN-CDP) on 18 February, citing uncertainty in the global economy, geopolitical tensions, changes in international trade, energy and supply chain risks, and domestic economic challenges.
The UN-CDP subsequently recommended that the request be favourably considered, while the United Nations Economic and Social Council (ECOSOC) recommended on 21 July that the General Assembly take a decision on the matter.
The extension will allow Bangladesh to retain the opportunity to benefit from LDC-related international support measures and trade preferences during the additional preparatory period, subject to the rules and arrangements of individual development partners and markets.
Economists and trade analysts have cautioned, however, that the additional time will not automatically resolve the challenges associated with graduation. Bangladesh will need to implement reforms and improve competitiveness before the revised deadline.

...

Bangladesh was included in the UN's LDC category in December 1975. Progress in agriculture, industrial development, garment exports, remittances, education and healthcare helped the country qualify for graduation.
The country met the UN's three graduation criteria – gross national income per capita, the human assets index, and the economic and environmental vulnerability index – in consecutive reviews in 2018 and 2021.
On 26 February 2021, the UN-CDP recommended Bangladesh's graduation. ECOSOC endorsed the recommendation on 8 June, and the General Assembly agreed to the transition on 24 November that year.
Bangladesh was granted a five-year preparatory period, rather than the usual three years, in consideration of the economic effects of the Covid-19 pandemic. Its graduation was consequently scheduled for 24 November 2026.

...

As an LDC, Bangladesh benefits from preferential market access in several major export destinations. Following graduation, changes to these arrangements could raise tariffs on Bangladeshi goods and weaken their price competitiveness in markets such as the European Union, the UK, Canada and Japan.

...

A 2025 analysis by the United Nations Conference on Trade and Development (UNCTAD) estimated that Bangladesh's total exports could decline by around 32% if it loses the relevant trade preferences. The estimate represents a potential impact, not a guaranteed outcome.
Mustafa Abid Khan, chief executive officer of the Bangladesh Foreign Trade Institute, told TBS that the extension could create an important opportunity for Bangladesh, particularly in intellectual property and trade negotiations.
He said the country would gain additional time to prepare for changes in intellectual property rules and pursue FTAs, while implementing necessary reforms in business and the wider economy.
"Under the current arrangements, we will lose many benefits anyway if business continues as usual. We must use the additional time effectively to implement the necessary reforms. If we make proper use of this period, many of our future problems can be reduced.

...

The government sought additional time amid uncertainty over global trade, geopolitical conflicts, energy supplies and domestic economic weaknesses. Other challenges include attracting investment, creating jobs, controlling inflation, increasing revenue collection and reforming the financial sector.
Over the next three years, Bangladesh will need to pursue alternative market-access arrangements and advance trade negotiations with major export destinations, including the European Union.
