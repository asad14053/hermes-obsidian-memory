## Ranking

### 1. Treatment 1 — Crossfire: permission versus law

Strongest immediate public-interest treatment and clearest central question. Attribution is generally disciplined: TIB’s position, newspaper reporting, the police clarification, the association’s defence, and the minister’s response remain distinguishable. The repeated warning that no new policy, killing, or operational order has been established is essential.

The main factual gap is also fundamental: the full IGP recording and original police clarification were not obtained. Articles 31–32 support a constitutional-limits discussion, but they do not alone settle every operational use-of-force question. The treatment is fairer than the headline controversy because it includes lawful-policing arguments, yet several modules restate the same permission-versus-law distinction.

Legal and reputational risk is high. Hooks 1, 2 and 8 could imply an adopted killing policy if captions, thumbnails, or edits drop their qualifiers. “বিচার ছাড়া হত্যা” is accurate as the legal issue being tested, not as a finding about government conduct.

Specific edits:

- Change Reel 2’s hook to: “আইজিপির বক্তব্য, পুলিশের ব্যাখ্যা ও সরকারের অবস্থান—কোথায় পার্থক্য?”
- Merge Reels 7–9 into one “দাবি, সিদ্ধান্ত ও অনুপস্থিত নথি” module.
- Keep “প্রতিবেদন অনুযায়ী” and “নতুন হত্যার ঘটনা প্রমাণ করছি না” in audio, not only graphics.
- Describe proportionality and lethal-force standards as attributed principles pending qualified review.
- Remove any title, caption, or thumbnail suggesting a “crossfire licence.”

Production readiness: closest to production, but not release-ready.

Release gates:

1. Refresh all responses and later administrative developments.
2. Seek the complete recording and original clarification; disclose clearly if unavailable.
3. Obtain Bangladesh-qualified legal review of the final script.
4. Offer a meaningful response opportunity to police/government subjects.
5. Time every standalone cut and verify that qualifiers survive the edit.

### 2. Treatment 2 — Malaysia FTA

Best evergreen explainer and lowest defamation risk. It correctly separates negotiations, a reported Terms of Reference, a future agreement, implementation, and measurable effects. Official expectations are identified as forecasts rather than outcomes.

The largest gaps are the signed ToR, round outcome, draft text, tariff schedule, rules of origin, impact assessment, and current LDC-graduation status. Fairness would improve with clearly labelled perspectives from domestic producers, exporters, workers, consumers, and Malaysian counterparts; these should not be invented merely to manufacture opposition.

Hook accuracy is mostly strong. Reel 8’s jobs/visa warning is useful but somewhat peripheral unless audience misinformation is documented. Reel 9’s delegation-count discrepancy is editorially sound but too minor for a priority reel. Reels 3–6 and 10 overlap around “terms before benefits.”

Specific edits:

- Replace Reel 1’s “কাল থেকেই” with the less theatrical “এখনই কি শুল্ক কমছে?”
- Merge product coverage, origin rules, and customs usability into one document-led module.
- Drop the delegation-number reel unless it illustrates a broader sourcing lesson.
- Treat the 2027 date consistently as an objective, never a deadline or forecast.
- Avoid any generic stages graphic that might imply an authenticated Bangladesh approval pathway.

Production readiness: suitable after the negotiation round, provided it remains a process explainer.

Release gates:

1. Obtain the round summary and signed ToR, or state explicitly that neither was obtained.
2. Refresh graduation/deferment status on recording day.
3. Obtain trade-specialist review for rules-of-origin and implementation explanations.
4. Prohibit unsupported tariff, employment, migration, price, and export forecasts.
5. Update the title if talks materially advance, stall, or change scope.

### 3. Treatment 3 — CPB confrontation

Editorially responsible but least ready. It fairly preserves the activists’ allegations, police denial, later protest, and limits of available evidence. The treatment avoids assigning party-wide or individual guilt.

The decisive gaps are incident footage, complaint records, medical evidence, police logs, suspect responses, custody information, and investigation outcome. Until those exist, ten reels would amplify an unresolved allegation more than illuminate it. Repeated formulations—“claim versus denial,” “evidence needed,” and “not yet verified”—create substantial redundancy.

Legal and reputational risk is highest for identification, injury claims, police conduct, and partisan coordination. Even question-form hooks can insinuate culpability. Reel 7’s slogan discussion may distract from the incident and trigger an unnecessary legal-status dispute.

Specific edits:

- Compress the treatment to two possible reels: competing accounts and investigation follow-up.
- Remove Reel 7 unless current legal status is separately verified and directly material.
- Do not show identifiable alleged participants, medical records, or party branding as culpability shorthand.
- Replace “হামলাকারী” with “অভিযুক্ত ব্যক্তি/গোষ্ঠী” unless independently established.
- Seek responses from police, complainants, named accused persons, and an appropriate party representative.

Production readiness: research treatment only; defer absent meaningful follow-up.

Release gates:

1. Obtain incident records or publish only a transparent missing-evidence audit.
2. Secure rights and authenticate any video used.
3. Obtain all material responses and preserve the police denial in every standalone cut.
4. Conduct legal review for identification and allegation wording.
5. Cancel rather than pad if no substantive update emerges.
