## 1. Banking distress, energy shortages and the World Bank growth warning

**CLASSIFICATION: A — IMMEDIATE VIDEO CANDIDATE**

1. **What exactly happened?** The Daily Star reported that a Bangladesh Development Update released on 6 October projected 3.4% growth for FY2026–27 and attributed the weak outlook to banking fragility, energy shortages and external shocks. It reported NPLs at 33.2% of loans in June, private-credit growth at 4.5%, and Tk760 billion in uncollateralised support to weak banks.[1]
2. **Original source?** The claimed original is the World Bank’s October Bangladesh Development Update, but its report/PDF and primary tables were not publicly retrievable by the cutoff. The Daily Star article is therefore the current traceable source for the October figures, not a substitute for the missing report.[1]
3. **Government position?** No Finance Ministry, Planning Ministry or Bangladesh Bank response to the October update was located by the cutoff. The Daily Star reports that Bangladesh Bank had cut its policy rate to 9.5% and reinstated a 4% lending-spread cap; those policy actions do not answer the World Bank’s critique.[1]
4. **Independent media source?** The Daily Star report is independent Bangladesh media and names the World Bank report as its underlying source. It independently reports the release but does not independently validate the World Bank’s calculations.[1]
5. **Historical context?** The World Bank’s official April update projected 3.9% growth in FY2026 and put the NPL ratio at 30.6% in December 2025.[2] The apparent deterioration is important, but the exact forecast-period comparison must be checked against the missing October table.
6. **Official numbers?** Reported October figures are 3.4% growth, 33.2% NPLs, 4.5% private-credit growth and Tk760 billion in liquidity support.[1] The April World Bank release provides a verified earlier baseline of 3.9% growth and 30.6% NPLs.[2]
7. **Is this genuinely new?** Yes: the October forecast and the World Bank’s combined banking-energy assessment are new. The underlying deterioration in NPLs and credit growth was already a continuing story.[1][2]
8. **Already heavily covered?** The broader banking crisis has been heavily covered; the fresh editorial value is the forecast revision and whether the October World Bank numbers reconcile with Bangladesh Bank series.[1][2]
9. **Visual/data angle?** Build an indexed timeline of World Bank growth forecasts and a separate NPL/private-credit chart. Mark every observation date and definition; do not put forecasts and observed outcomes on one unlabeled line.
10. **What remains unproven?** The exact October table, the definition behind 33.2%, the period and recipient set behind Tk760 billion, and the causal contribution of energy shortages to the growth forecast.[1] Obtain the report before scripting definitive numerical claims.

**Editorial decision:** Start reporting now because the public-interest and data-discrepancy angles are strong. Publication should wait for the October report/PDF and Bangladesh Bank source tables.

## 2. Threatened Chattogram Port shutdown and the NCT concession

**CLASSIFICATION: A — IMMEDIATE VIDEO CANDIDATE**

1. **What exactly happened?** Port workers said on 5 October that they would halt operations at Chattogram Port and the Kutubdia outer anchorage if an NCT agreement were signed. This was a conditional threat; no shutdown had occurred by the cutoff.[7]
2. **Original source?** The immediate event is documented through TBS’s report of the workers’ sit-in and statements.[7] For the underlying project, the PPP Authority’s public profile records a G2G concession plan involving DP World, a 23 March 2023 in-principle approval and an estimated US$205 million feasibility-study cost.[6] The 1 October 2026 CCEA minute and draft concession were not publicly located.
3. **Government position?** The PPP Authority says the NCT/OCY project remains “in development” and identifies CPA, IFC’s advisory role and the planned G2G structure.[6] Independent reporting says the CCEA approved a 15-year draft concession on 1 October but did not disclose an operator in that approval.[8]
4. **Independent media source?** TBS independently covered the 5 October threat.[7] The Daily Star separately covered the 1 October draft-concession approval and reported that the committee action did not name an operator.[8]
5. **Historical context?** The official PPP page dates the first in-principle approval to March 2023.[6] The Daily Star reports that the operator process began in 2019, was suspended after worker action before the February 2026 election, and later resumed.[8]
6. **Official numbers?** The PPP Authority lists US$205 million as the estimated cost according to the feasibility study.[6] The Daily Star reports a 15-year concession period, while worker-platform revenue and income figures remain advocacy claims until checked against audited CPA accounts.[8]
7. **Is this genuinely new?** Yes: the 1 October draft-concession approval and 5 October shutdown threat are new. Foreign-operation plans themselves are longstanding.[7][8]
8. **Already heavily covered?** The concession decision has received substantial national coverage.[8] The escalation to a port-wide and outer-anchorage shutdown threat is the fresh element.[7]
9. **Visual/data angle?** Map NCT, OCY, CCT, GCB and the outer anchorage; chart terminal-level TEUs, revenue, vessel dwell time and crane availability; and build a 2019–2026 decision-and-protest timeline. Keep NCT/OCY separate from CCT/GCB.
10. **What remains unproven?** Final operator, tariff/revenue-share terms, capital obligations, labour protections, security provisions, comparative public-versus-private returns, and whether the threatened shutdown will occur.[6][7][8] The draft concession and CCEA record are the essential documents.

**Editorial decision:** Deploy document requests and port reporting immediately. Frame the video around undisclosed concession terms and shutdown risk, not claims that a transfer has already been completed.

## 3. Furnace-oil sludge entering the Sangu River system

**CLASSIFICATION: A — IMMEDIATE VIDEO CANDIDATE**

1. **What exactly happened?** The Department of Environment told TBS it had inspected the Dohazari-Kaliaish power plant and affected area, collected water samples, ordered polluted water stopped, issued a notice and begun an enforcement process. The plant manager acknowledged that some water-mixed furnace-oil sludge escaped during heavy rain and flooding and said roughly 400,000 litres remained stored at the plant.[13]
2. **Original source?** The primary records should be the DoE notice, inspection file, sampling chain-of-custody and laboratory results; none was publicly located by the cutoff. TBS is the traceable source for the 6 October enforcement update and attributed official statements.[13]
3. **Government position?** DoE said samples were sent to its regional office and further action would proceed through enforcement. The BPDB plant manager said the discharge route had been closed, cleanup had begun and stored sludge would be removed through auction.[13]
4. **Independent media source?** Dhaka Post published field reporting on 29 September, describing oily material moving from the plant’s southern drain toward Moillya canal and quoting the manager acknowledging that some sludge had entered the canal.[14]
5. **Historical context?** The pollution allegation and site observations predated the 6 October enforcement report; the new development is the notice/enforcement stage and disclosed stored-sludge estimate.[13][14]
6. **Official numbers?** The only current quantity is the plant manager’s estimate of about 400,000 litres stored; it is an official claim, not an independently measured volume. TBS reports an approximately three-kilometre canal route to the Sangu.[13]
7. **Is this genuinely new?** Yes. The enforcement update is material and the reported stored quantity creates a testable waste-management and procurement trail.[13]
8. **Already heavily covered?** No. One identified Bangla field report preceded the TBS enforcement update; this remains underdeveloped rather than saturated.[13][14]
9. **Visual/data angle?** Film and map the plant drain–Moillya canal–Sangu route; photograph cleanup and remaining storage; test water and sediment; and trace oily-water-treatment and sludge-removal contracts. Map agricultural plots only after land records and verified impact evidence are obtained.
10. **What remains unproven?** The 400,000-litre figure, discharged volume and duration, laboratory exceedances, crop/fish/health effects, compliance failures, and legal liability.[13][14] A notice and enforcement process are not a final violation finding.[13]

**Editorial decision:** This is the strongest field investigation: visible route, acknowledged escape, regulatory action, local impacts to test and a procurement/document trail. Obtain the DoE records before alleging statutory violations.

## 4. HRW challenge to the mass detention of Jasad supporters

**CLASSIFICATION: B — WATCH / WAIT FOR MORE EVIDENCE**

1. **What exactly happened?** HRW issued a 5 October statement describing the 26 September detention of around 290 Jasad meeting attendees and the later custody of 27 as political repression. That is HRW’s rights assessment, not a court ruling that the detention was unlawful.[3]
2. **Original source?** HRW’s statement is the original source for the 5 October criticism.[3] The underlying event was independently reported on 27 September, when police said 289 people had been taken to a station for verification.[4]
3. **Government position?** Police told The Daily Star they acted after receiving information about a gathering of a banned organisation and an allegation that “Joy Bangla” slogans were chanted; at the time, they said identities and possible offences were still being checked.[4] No specific government response to HRW’s 5 October criticism was located.
4. **Independent media source?** The Daily Star’s event report included both named police accounts and Jasad’s competing account.[4] Its later report said 27 were sent to jail after police produced them in court.[5]
5. **Historical context?** The detention occurred on 26 September, with court proceedings and releases reported over the next two days; the HRW statement is a later interpretation and advocacy intervention.[3][4][5]
6. **Official numbers?** Police told The Daily Star that 289 people were taken to the station, while 27 were later sent to jail.[4][5] Public reporting does not provide a custody register that conclusively reconciles detention and release counts.
7. **Is this genuinely new?** Partly. HRW’s intervention is new; the arrests, police rationale and custody proceedings were already public.[3][4][5]
8. **Already heavily covered?** Yes. The mass detention and immediate court process were covered in late September.[4][5] A fresh video needs new records or consequences, not a recap of HRW’s statement.
9. **Visual/data angle?** Build a custody-and-procedure timeline and reconcile the headcount from police station records, GD/FIR, forwarding report, court order and release list. Show uncertainty rather than forcing one total.
10. **What remains unproven?** Attendees’ affiliations, whether slogans were chanted, whether an offence or sabotage plan existed, whether force was used, and whether detention was arbitrary or unlawful.[3][4][5] Also avoid describing jail custody as police remand unless the court order supports that term.

**Editorial decision:** Hold. Escalate to A only after obtaining the GD/FIR, forwarding report, custody/release register, court order and a Home Ministry or DMP response.

## 5. Proposed 442MW Rampal solar plant

**CLASSIFICATION: B — WATCH / WAIT FOR MORE EVIDENCE**

1. **What exactly happened?** TBS reported that BPDB’s proposed 442MWp Rampal solar project was expected to go before ECNEC on 7 October. The proposal included a reported Tk2,502.39 crore cost, Tk6.18/kWh tariff, 685-acre usable site and December 2030 completion target. It was not an ECNEC approval at the cutoff.[10]
2. **Original source?** The underlying primary document is BPDB’s DPP/project proposal, which TBS says it reviewed, but no public DPP or ECNEC agenda was located.[10]
3. **Government position?** BSS, which counts as official-government-position reporting, quoted the BPDB chair on 12 September saying ECNEC approval was expected that week and described the project as aligned with national renewable-energy targets.[11] The earlier missed expectation makes the new meeting date a forecast, not confirmation.[10][11]
4. **Independent media source?** TBS independently reported the project proposal and attributed the figures to the proposal it reviewed.[10]
5. **Historical context?** The proposal had already passed Planning Commission/PEC processing and had been publicly presented as nearing approval in September.[11] The project details are therefore not wholly new.[10][11]
6. **Official numbers?** Reported proposal figures are 442MWp, 685 usable acres, Tk2,502.39 crore total cost, Tk2,127 crore from the Power Development Fund, Tk375.94 crore from BPDB, Tk6.18/kWh tariff and about three kilometres of new 230kV transmission line.[10] These are proposed figures, not approved allocations or contract prices.
7. **Is this genuinely new?** Only the reported 7 October ECNEC scheduling is new. Capacity, location, tariff and financing concepts were already public.[10][11]
8. **Already heavily covered?** Yes. The project had prior official-position and independent coverage before this scan.[10][11]
9. **Visual/data angle?** Map the 685-acre footprint within Rampal Block B, green belt, flood-protection and water features; compare cost/MWp, tariff and capacity factor with Sonagazi; and diagram grid evacuation.[10] Use the final DPP, not media estimates, for definitive comparisons.
10. **What remains unproven?** Whether ECNEC will consider or approve the project on 7 October, final schedule, tariff methodology, procurement route, environmental assessment, grid-integration cost and the “largest” claim under consistent AC/DC definitions.[10][11]

**Editorial decision:** Wait for the ECNEC outcome and public DPP. Promote to A only if approval changes the cost, schedule, procurement or environmental conditions, or if the meeting slips again.
