# Bangladeshis forced into war in Russia
The commission collected invitation letters, travel itineraries, flight tickets, visas, Russian entry documents, hotel bookings, military contracts and documents from the Russian Ministry of Defense. The commission sent 69 letters to various countries regarding this issue and received responses to six. The report noted that Russia did not respond to any of the commission's 41 written requests.

...

An independent UN commission of inquiry has found evidence that citizens of 29 countries, including Bangladesh, were recruited into the Russian armed forces through deception and coercion and sent to fight in Ukraine.
The commission stated that Russian authorities carried out this "human trafficking" in coordination with active recruiters and agents across various countries. The commission also obtained copies of business visas issued to Bangladeshi recruits, which cite invitations from a Russian civilian company.
These findings were detailed in a draft report published on the website of the Office of the UN High Commissioner for Human Rights (OHCHR) on 26 October.
The UN Independent International Commission of Inquiry on Ukraine prepared the report for presentation to the 81st session of the UN General Assembly.
The report states that the commission reviewed information regarding the recruitment of citizens from 48 countries into the Russian forces and found evidence of deception or coercion involving nationals from 29 countries.

Earlier, on 21 October, Prothom Alo published a report titled, " [Lured to Russia by promises of jobs, 54 Bangladeshis killed in war in 3 years](https://en.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/pqmoumnzr2) ." That report revealed that at least 54 Bangladeshis had died on the battlefield after going to Russia. It also highlighted allegations that individuals were lured with job offers only to be sent to the front lines.
Commenting on the investigation report, Erik Møse, Chair of the UN Commission of Inquiry, issued a statement on the OHCHR website. He noted that the commission’s investigations found that Russia had coordinated with agents operating around the world to traffic citizens of 29 countries into the Russian armed forces and send them to the front lines in Ukraine.

...

However, quoting a statement from the Russian mission to the UN, various international media outlets reported on Monday that Russia does not recognise the jurisdiction of this commission of inquiry. Moscow claims the report is based on fabricated information.
Expatriates' Welfare and Overseas Employment Minister Ariful Haque Choudhury told Prothom Alo, "Extra precautions are being taken regarding sending workers to Russia. Instructions have been issued to scrutinise appointment letters much more rigorously now. Action has already been initiated against the recruiting agencies involved. The government has taken a strict stance on this matter."

## High-paying civilian job offers
According to the UN commission's report, men living abroad received job offers in Russia through social media advertisements or via recruitment agents and agencies. The commission noted that they were offered roles as security guards, drivers, mechanics, electricians, construction workers, welders, repairmen, transport workers and technicians.

...

## Sent to the battlefield after minimal training
According to the UN report, basic military training lasted anywhere from 20 minutes to a few days. In some cases, training went on for up to a month. After that, they were deployed to various battlefields in Ukraine.

...

The report stated that the commission utilised 245 testimonies for its investigation into the recruitment process. These included accounts from recruits held by Ukrainian authorities or those who managed to return home, their family members, lawyers, local authorities, researchers and journalists.

The commission of inquiry also issued several recommendations. It urged Russia to cease recruiting foreign nationals through deception or coercion, discharge the trafficked individuals currently in its forces, and provide them with assistance. The commission further recommended investigating and prosecuting those involved in the recruitment process.
Selim Reza, Coordinator of the Center for Migration Studies at North South University, told Prothom Alo, "While the Russian labour market may hold potential, the market demand was not assessed properly."

...

Selim Reza further said, "This must be prevented through diplomatic engagement with Russia. Emphasis must be placed on ensuring that not a single worker from the country is deceived or forced into the conflict. Compensation also must be secured for those affected and arrangements must be made to rescue those trapped in the war there."
