## [1] TBS: BPC deposit investigation

URL: https://tbsnews.net/economy/energy/bpc-launches-probe-tk4813cr-deposits-troubled-private-banks-1595626
Published: 2026-11-07

> The Bangladesh Petroleum Corporation (BPC) has formed a seven-member investigation committee to examine Tk4,813.32 crore deposited by the state-owned corporation and its subsidiaries in financially distressed private banks, amid concerns over possible irregularities and the safety of public funds.

> It will also examine whether the transactions involved personal benefits, bribes or commissions through unethical dealings, particularly those linked to unusually high interest rate offers from the banks.

> According to an office order issued on 3 November and signed by BPC Secretary Shahina Sultana, the committee has also been tasked with recommending measures to recover the funds held in fixed deposit receipts (FDRs) at the troubled banks.

> The committee has been instructed to submit a comprehensive report to the BPC chairman within one month of its formation.

## [2] The Daily Star: Tk4,500 crore loan decision

URL: https://www.thedailystar.net/news/bangladesh/news/after-11yrs-bpc-gets-tk-4500cr-loan-buy-fuel-4286591
Published: 2026-09-30

> After an 11-year gap, the government is set to provide Bangladesh Petroleum Corporation (BPC) with a Tk 4,500 crore interest-free loan to help ensure uninterrupted fuel supplies.

> The Finance Division informed the Energy and Mineral Resources Division of the decision in a letter yesterday, saying the money would be allocated from the operational loan sector of the budget for the current fiscal year.

> The loan will have to be repaid in half-yearly installments over five years, including a six-month grace period. The BPC will have to sign a formal loan agreement with the Finance Division, and the money can be used only for approved fuel-related expenses.

> The financing will help the BPC meet its immediate fuel-import obligations, as the corporation had earlier warned that it could run out of funds to open letters of credit for fuel imports as early as October unless it received fresh financing.

## [3] The Daily Star: earlier deposit lock-up

URL: https://online.thedailystar.net/business/news/unable-return-deposits-7-banks-leave-bpc-dry-3828916
Published: 2025-02-19

> The Bangladesh Petroleum Corporation (BPC) says it cannot encash deposits worth nearly Tk 1,700 crore held with seven private banks, which the government agency blames for causing delays to its development projects and disrupting regular operations.

> The BPC's investments with the banks, amounting to Tk 1,677.23 crore, are in the form of fixed deposits receipts (FDRs) and short-notice deposits. This means interest is also payable on these investments.

> "We are trying to liquidate the deposits we have in various banks. Our balance in these banks could exceed several thousand crores of taka," Md Amin Ul Ahsan, chairman of the BPC, told The Daily Star.

> "Due to a liquidity crisis, we are unable to encash the FDRs at the moment," said Mosharraf Hossain, manager of the FSIB's Agrabad branch.

## [4] BSS: loan reported as provided

URL: https://www.bssnews.net/news-flash/430044
Published: 2026-09-30

> The government has provided a loan of Tk 4,500 crore to Bangladesh Petroleum Corporation (BPC) to meet the cost of importing petroleum products and related expenses amid global fuel crisis, officials said today.

> BPC Chairman Dr Md Rafiqul Islam told BSS today that the corporation received the amount as it sought Tk 5,000 crore as loan from the government.

> BPC needed the fresh government loan to meet its fuel import financing requirements, maintain uninterrupted supply of petroleum products and enhance its capacity to open letters of credit (LCs) for imports.
