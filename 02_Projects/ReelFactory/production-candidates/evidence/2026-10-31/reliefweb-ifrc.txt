# Bangladesh Measles - DREF Operation Update (MDRBD039) - October 2026

- **Posted** 22 Oct 2026
- **Originally published** 22 Oct 2026

Bangladesh continues to experience a large-scale measles outbreak, with sustained transmission reported across all eight divisions of the country. Between 15 March and 14 October 2026, a cumulative 208,570 suspected measles cases, 22,840 laboratory-conrmed cases, and 185,390 measles-related hospital admissions were reported nationwide. During the reporting period, the outbreak continued to place signicant pressure on health facilities, with approximately 1,000 suspected measles admissions reported daily and a peak of 1,251 admissions recorded on 8 October 2026. Measles-associated mortality remains a major concern, with 1,073 suspected deaths and 104 conrmed deaths reported since the beginning of the outbreak. Dhaka Division continues to bear the highest burden, followed by Chattogram and Barishal divisions.

In Cox’s Bazar Cox’s Bazar Rohingya Camps, measles transmission remains active despite recent declines in reported cases. During Epidemiological Week (EW) 40, 149 suspected cases were reported, down from 301 cases at the peak in EW37. Since the start of 2026, 2,939 suspected cases, including 2,344 conrmed cases and 12 deaths, have been reported across the Rohingya camps. While the trend is encouraging, continued vaccination, surveillance, and community engagement eorts remain critical to address remaining immunity gaps and prevent further transmission among refugee and host communities.

The outbreak has been driven by accumulated immunity gaps resulting from years of missed vaccination opportunities and suboptimal immunization coverage. While Bangladesh has implemented multiple emergency and follow-up measles-rubella (MR) vaccination campaigns, a substantial number of children remain susceptible to infection, particularly unvaccinated and under-immunized children, infants below the age of routine immunization, and children living in underserved and hard-to-reach communities. Since April 2026, nearly 20 million children have been vaccinated through emergency and follow-up campaigns; however, ongoing transmission indicates that signicant immunity gaps persist.

To address remaining susceptibility, the Ministry of Health launched a nationwide MR vaccination campaign on 26 September 2026 targeting approximately 3.4 million unvaccinated and missed children, prioritizing high-risk areas including Dhaka and Cox's Bazar.

Despite administrative reports indicating high campaign coverage, transmission has continued in several locations, underscoring the challenges of reaching all eligible children and achieving population-level immunity.
 On 14 October 2026, a Joint Independent Review Team comprising representatives from WHO, UNICEF, Gavi, the Ministry of Health, EPI, and national public health experts concluded that the outbreak continues to be driven by signicant immunity gaps accumulated over several years. The review estimated eective vaccination coverage at 78-84 per cent, below the 95-96 per cent threshold required to interrupt transmission, and noted an increasing proportion of cases among children aged 5-9 years. The review further found that active transmission is now concentrated in approximately 120 high-burden upazilas and recommended prioritizing these areas for intensied vaccination, surveillance, and community engagement eorts.

Since the beginning of the outbreak, the Bangladesh Red Crescent Society (BDRCS), supported by the International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies (IFRC) through the DREF operation, has been working closely with the government and health partners to support outbreak control eorts. Through the deployment of more than 1,200 volunteers, BDRCS has contributed to community mobilization, household visits, identication and referral of zero-dose and under-immunized children, crowd management at vaccination sites, community-based surveillance, and risk communication activities aimed at reducing transmission and increasing vaccine uptake in high-risk communities.
