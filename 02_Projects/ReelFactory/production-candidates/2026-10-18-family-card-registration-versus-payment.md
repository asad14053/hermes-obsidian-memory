## CORE THESIS

Editorial thesis: a large database is not the same thing as a large number of paid beneficiaries. The useful test is whether an eligible household can get its information recorded correctly, understand the selection decision and correct an error.

The official SSPS overview describes targeted financial assistance and PMT-based selection; October reporting separately gives a nationwide database target and a smaller selected pilot group.[1][2]
Those sources support the distinction. They do not establish how many deserving households were wrongly excluded, or whether a correction process works.

## COUNTERARGUMENT / ALTERNATIVE VIEW

The strongest alternative is that a broad registry and a common selection method are sensible ways to administer targeted support. The government's overview presents integration and digital transfer as intended benefits.[1]
A database that includes more households than the payment programme is not, by itself, evidence of deception or failure. A pilot selection difference is also not proof that poor families were excluded unfairly.

Our response should be a test, not a dismissal: ask for the current criteria, documented correction route and independently checked payment outcomes. Avoid promising that any scoring system will remove political influence. BSS attributes that assurance to the minister; it is a claim, not a verified result.[4]
