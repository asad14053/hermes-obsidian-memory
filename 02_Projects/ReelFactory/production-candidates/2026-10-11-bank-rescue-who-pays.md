## CORE THESIS

Editorial thesis: protecting depositors is a legitimate objective, but a funding request should come with a bank-by-bank loss estimate, a rule for who absorbs losses, and a plan for recovering public money. These are questions to test the proposal against, not verified features of it.

The IMF's January 2026 assessment called for estimates of undercapitalization, defined fiscal support and legally robust restructuring and resolution plans.[24]
The FSB's resolution standard aims to preserve critical services while allocating losses according to the creditor hierarchy, with equity absorbing losses first.[22]
Use those as analytical benchmarks. They do not establish what Bangladesh's current law requires or whether this particular draft complies.

### Calculations and internal validation

Conditional, undiscounted arithmetic, computed in Python:

- `60,000 crore/year × 5 years = 300,000 crore = 3 lakh crore`.
- `60,000 crore/year × 8 years = 480,000 crore = 4.8 lakh crore`.

The input amount and duration come from the reported draft request.[1]
This is a constant-annual-allocation scenario. It is not an approved bill, a forecast of net taxpayer loss, a present-value estimate, or an amount proved stolen. Recovery proceeds, dividends, financing costs, timing and overlap with existing assistance are unresolved. Never add the existing capital and liquidity figures to this scenario without reconciling scope and accounting.

No per-person “bill,” GDP share, number of hospitals or Padma Bridge comparison is used: those need additional denominators and like-for-like cost assumptions. The reported nonperforming-loan stock is not used as a proxy for theft or the capital shortfall. No guilt finding is inferred from the governor's rhetoric.

## COUNTERARGUMENT / ALTERNATIVE VIEW

The strongest objection to a taxpayer-focused critique is that refusing support can leave depositors and critical payment services exposed. The FSB benchmark itself includes continuity of vital services and arrangements for temporary funding; it is not a demand to abandon depositors.[22]

Our response is therefore conditional: judge the rescue design, not just the headline sum. The IMF's Bangladesh assessment calls for a defined fiscal role alongside bank valuations and legally robust resolution plans.[24]
A well-designed intervention could be preferable to a disorderly failure. This brief does not estimate those competing costs or decide whether the reported amount is too high or too low.

Give this counterargument real screen time before the final verdict. Avoid both “no public money under any circumstances” and “any public money is justified if called depositor protection.”
