## KEY EVIDENCE

### E1: Bangladesh is named, and the visa detail is specific

The Commission says it investigated recruitment from 48 countries and found evidence of deception or coercion involving nationals from 29 countries, including Bangladesh (A/81/440, paras 37-38).[1]

Paragraph 49 says the Commission received copies of business visas given to Bangladeshi recruits and that the visas mentioned an invitation from a civilian company in Russia.[1]

Evidence boundary: the Bangladesh-specific line establishes the visa detail.[1] The broader recruitment funnel is a cross-country finding and should stay labelled that way.[1]

### E2: The Commission used testimony and documents

The report says the Commission relied on 245 testimonies and collected invitation letters, travel records, visas and Russian Ministry of Defence contracts or records (A/81/440, para 45).[1]

This is stronger than a single anecdote, but it is not a complete census of every affected Bangladeshi.[1][3][4]

### E3: The recruitment pattern includes coercion after arrival

The Commission describes false civilian job offers, rapid travel arrangements and compelled Russian-language military contracts after arrival (A/81/440, paras 40, 49 and 53).[1]

Paragraph 53 also says phones, passports and driving licences were often taken. Do not present that stage as Bangladesh-specific unless a Bangladesh case record is obtained.[1]

### E4: The exact Bangladesh total is unresolved

Prothom Alo says the report does not provide the number of Bangladesh visas, recruited people, identities or fates.[3] The Daily Star separately notes that the UN report does not specify how many Bangladeshis were trafficked, killed or missing.[4]

Any media, NGO, police or employment-clearance count needs its own scope, date and deduplication.[unverified] None should be substituted for a UN Bangladesh total.[3][4]

### E5: Bangladesh law creates an information baseline

Section 26 of the Overseas Employment and Migrants Act 2013 says a migrant worker has a right, before departure, to information about the migration process, employment contract, work conditions and legal rights.[5]

This supports a public-accountability question.[5] A final script still needs Bangladesh-qualified legal review before applying offences, liability or remedies to a named case.[unverified]

### E6: A denial and government response must be carried accurately

Prothom Alo reports that Russia's UN mission rejects the Commission's jurisdiction and says the report is based on fabricated information.[3]

The same report attributes to Bangladesh's expatriates' welfare minister a statement that appointment-letter scrutiny was being tightened and action had begun against involved recruiting agencies.[3] The underlying orders, agency files and case records were not retrieved, so the script must keep this as attributed reporting until the documents are obtained.[unverified]

## COUNTERARGUMENT / ALTERNATIVE VIEW

Some foreign nationals joined armed forces knowingly and voluntarily. The Commission says it excluded those situations and focused on recruitment that violated international law (A/81/440, para 37).[1]

Russia also has legitimate civilian employment.[3] A business visa, travel to Russia or a recruitment agency is not, by itself, proof of trafficking.[1] The story concerns the Commission's documented deception-and-coercion pattern and the Bangladesh-specific visa evidence, not every Russia-bound worker.[1]

Prothom Alo reports that Russia's UN mission rejects the Commission's jurisdiction and calls the reporting fabricated.[3] A fair video should state that denial and then show the Commission's disclosed method, documents and unanswered requests. It should not treat either side's assertion as self-proving.
