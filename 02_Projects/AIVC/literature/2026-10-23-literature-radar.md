## 1. READ NOW — DynaVelo: journal-publication update

**Citation:** Karbalayghareh A, Pelzer B, Chin CR, Melnick A, Barisic D, Leslie CS. Deep dynamical models of single-cell multiomic velocities predict loss-of-function and rescue perturbations in B cells. Nature Biotechnology (2026). Version of record October 21, 2026. DOI: 10.1038/s41587-026-03348-1.
**Source:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-026-03348-1
**PDF:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-026-03348-1.pdf
**Freshness:** New journal version of an older preprint (10.1101/2025.04.24.650458). Publication day verified; exact time relative to cutoff unresolved. No claim that the rescue experiments first appeared in this journal version; an older June preprint abstract already describes them. PDF retains a placeholder online-publication date; use publisher HTML's explicit version-of-record date.

**Research problem:** Infer multimodal cellular dynamics and intervention targets rather than simply order cells along a trajectory.
**Method:** A variational latent neural ODE jointly models RNA and chromVAR-derived TF motif accessibility, guided by partial RNA velocities. Decoders recover expression/motif vector fields. Jacobians and finite in silico input perturbations yield cell-state-specific regulatory hypotheses and predicted rescue targets. [Main; Results: DynaVelo learns a latent neural ODE; Fig. 1.]
**Key result:** In murine germinal-center B cells, the model nominated Ctcf, Stat3 and Bcl6 to rescue an Arid1a-loss phenotype. Double-heterozygous mouse experiments confirmed rescue of the dark-zone phenotype for all three; Arid1aHet;CtcfHet multiome profiling supported the predicted shift toward WT dynamics. These are phenotype-specific results, not rescue of all disease features. [Abstract; Fig. 6; Discussion.]
**What is new here:** Joint RNA–motif dynamical modeling with an explicitly perturbable vector field and experimentally tested rescue predictions. Novelty is relative to earlier model classes; the new event this run is peer-reviewed publication, not first disclosure.
**Why it matters for AIVC:** A high-impact model-to-experiment template for a tightly scoped K99 aim. A spatial-context AIVC should demonstrate that context changes a useful prediction and improves experimental prioritization, not merely that it reconstructs expression or yields plausible edges.
**What is weak / unresolved:** Main validation is a specific mouse B-cell system, not mature human cortex or SCZ. Motif accessibility is a proxy for TF activity. Simulated KO clamps RNA/motif inputs to minima, while biological tests use heterozygous loss; this is not a calibrated universal dosage model. Snapshot-derived velocity guidance and latent-time assumptions do not uniquely identify all causal dynamics. Selected rescue successes do not establish genome-wide unseen-target generalization.
**Future research idea:** On an appropriate neural perturbation dataset with measured responses and preferably paired multiome, compare a DynaVelo-style RNA–ATAC baseline against a neighborhood-conditioned extension for prioritizing one SCZ-relevant rescue intervention. Test the added context on held-out donors and in an independent experiment. Do not infer this feasibility from existing postmortem spatial data alone.
**Decision:** READ NOW for experimental-validation design and grant positioning.

## 2. READ NOW — GeoSinkhorn Flow

**Citation:** Xu K, Wilkinson A, Ni S, Huguet G, Gerstein M, Wolf G, Tape CJ, Krishnaswamy S. GeoSinkhorn Flow: Geometry-Aware Flow Matching for Perturbation-Conditioned Dynamics in Single cell Data Phenoscapes. bioRxiv v1, posted October 22, 2026. DOI: 10.64898/2026.10.16.759724.
**Source:** https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.16.759724v1
**Dated PDF inspected:** https://www.biorxiv.org/content/biorxiv/early/2026/10/22/2026.10.16.759724.full.pdf
**Research problem:** Static maps of entire cell-state distributions do not specify continuous perturbation-conditioned transitions or transfer them to unseen conditions.
**Method:** Heat-diffusion geometry on a neighborhood graph is distilled into a geometric autoencoder. Geometry-aware Sinkhorn transport supervises conditional flow matching using drug/gene embeddings. An unbalanced transport variant models changing population mass as well as state movement. A phenoscape point denotes an experimental condition's cell distribution, not an individual cell. [Abstract; Introduction; Results 2.1.]
**Key result:** Authors report recovery of manifold geometry and generalization to held-out drug combinations in patient-derived organoids, held-out Perturb-seq targets, and time points in CyTOF signaling. No headline percentage is quoted because the complete comparative tables/split construction were not audited. [Abstract; Introduction; Results section headings.]
**What is new here:** Conditional population flows that preserve the geometry of distribution-level phenoscapes and can represent changing cell-state abundance; not merely applying Euclidean flow matching to cell embeddings.
**Why it matters for AIVC:** Direct competitor for the perturbation-response decoder and a reminder that changing composition and within-state expression are different endpoints. A biology-plus-perturbation graph transformer needs to beat an appropriate transport baseline rather than only other transformers.
**What is weak / unresolved:** For cross-sectional data, progression may be pseudotime/MELD ordering rather than measured biological time. Geometrically smooth paths are not observed lineage trajectories or uniquely identified mechanisms. The diffusion graph is a molecular-state graph, not automatically a spatial-neighbor graph. Audit whether geometry/autoencoder fitting excludes held-out conditions before interpreting generalization as fully inductive; no leakage finding is claimed here. Neural/SCZ performance is unestablished.
**Future research idea:** Compare spatially conditioned versus identity-only transport of neural perturbation responses, scoring both within-cell-type shifts and abundance changes. Fit all geometry on training donors/conditions and test against Euclidean flow matching and embedding-plus-kNN at matched resources.
**Decision:** READ NOW as a methodological comparator; does not justify replacing the planned simple pilot before context value is established.

## 3. READ NOW — Foundation Models Improve Perturbation Response Prediction: revised preprint

**Citation:** Cole E, Huizing GJ, Addagudi S, et al. Foundation Models Improve Perturbation Response Prediction. bioRxiv, revision posted October 22, 2026. DOI: 10.64898/2026.02.18.706454.
**Source:** https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.02.18.706454
**Dated revision PDF:** https://www.biorxiv.org/content/biorxiv/early/2026/10/22/2026.02.18.706454.full.pdf
**Freshness:** Revision, not first submission. Primary page states figures redrawn, text revised/expanded and additional results/new figures. Version number was not exposed reliably, so preserve the dated PDF and do not invent a v-number. Exact additions relative to the preceding revision were not diffed.
**Research problem:** Resolve apparently contradictory claims about whether pretrained representations beat simple perturbation predictors.
**Method:** Embedding-centric comparison spanning over 600 embedding-task pairs; expression, DNA, protein and prior-knowledge embeddings; genetic/chemical tasks; simple heads, generative comparators and attention-based fusion. [Abstract; Results 2.1–2.3.]
**Key result:** Prior-knowledge embeddings, including STRING GNN, DepMap and GenePT, are often strongest. On the Essential screens, complex distribution generators did not beat strongest-embedding kNN when judged by mean response. The authors report that benchmark gains depend strongly on reproducible perturbation signal; Norman yielded little improvement over Train Mean. [Results 2.1–2.2; Fig. 1.]
**What is new here:** Separates perturbation representation from prediction head and connects conflicting benchmark conclusions to data/signal quality. This run's new event is a materially expanded revision; individual findings are not asserted to be newly added without a version diff.
**Why it matters for AIVC:** Strongest immediate challenge to an architecture-first GNN/GAT/transformer proposal. Include prior-knowledge embeddings plus kNN/lasso before attributing gains to biological graph structure, attention or foundation-model scale. Gene embeddings used to identify perturbations are not interchangeable with the proposed scGPT/UCE cell-identity embeddings.
**What is weak / unresolved:** Distributional predictions were reduced to means for this comparison, so it cannot establish that generative models add no value for heterogeneity. The authors explicitly report less extensive tuning of expensive models despite greater GPU use. Predominant task is unseen perturbations in seen cell lines, not human-brain donor/context transfer. All authors declare GenBio AI affiliation; assess its own model comparisons accordingly, without treating affiliation as evidence of invalidity. Prior-knowledge coverage/exposure needs auditing in any new benchmark.
**Future research idea:** Run a factorized AIVC benchmark: fixed cell-state/context inputs; vary perturbation embedding source and response head separately. Evaluate held-out targets and donors, stratify by independently measured replicate reliability, and score both mean effects and full distributions. Test whether spatial context adds value beyond the best simple prior-knowledge baseline.
**Decision:** READ NOW before investing in a perturbation-trained transformer.

## 4. SAVE — Gate-Based Feature Importance in Simulation-Based Inference for EEG: A Cautionary Tale

**Citation:** Deshmukh S, Brenner S, Knösche TR, Scherf N. Gate-Based Feature Importance in Simulation-Based Inference for EEG: A Cautionary Tale. arXiv:2610.30364v1, October 21, 2026, 19:28:20 UTC. Non-archival acceptance reported at NeurIPS 2026 Sim2Science and Interp4Discovery workshops.
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30364v1
**Methods/results:** https://arxiv.org/html/2610.30364v1
**Research problem:** Whether cheap learned gates accurately identify which inputs matter for mechanistic parameter inference.
**Method:** Neural posterior estimation for four Jansen–Rit parameters from simulated EEG features; compare jointly trained sigmoid/softmax gates with leave-one-feature-out retraining across nine seeds. [Methods 2.1–2.3.]
**Key result:** Gate-derived rankings negatively correlate with ablation-derived importance: approximately −0.61 and −0.56. Total signal power is most important under retraining but strongly downweighted by gates; predictive performance can remain good despite misleading importance rankings. [Abstract; Results/Table 1.]
**What is new here:** A controlled SBI-specific counterexample showing reproducible gates can disagree with retrained predictive importance; the broader caution about attention is not new.
**Why it matters for AIVC:** Do not call a GAT/transformer attention map a causal GRN or environmental mechanism solely because the model predicts well. This is an analogy and validation lesson, not a direct test of the user's architecture.
**What is weak / unresolved:** One simulated EEG setting with hand-designed features, not single-cell gene networks. Retraining ablation measures predictive reliance under a learning procedure, not the causal effect of a biological intervention; correlated predictors can also complicate the interpretation of ablations. It does not prove that every attention explanation is invalid.
**Future research idea:** Build a graph-interpretability audit comparing attention edge/gene rankings to retrained gene-module removal, matched degree/coexpression controls, and independent measured CRISPR effects. Report prediction and mechanistic validity as separate outcomes.
**Decision:** SAVE for the interpretability/rigor section; use the lesson immediately, not as another architecture to implement.

## 5. SAVE — scRhythmBayes

**Citation:** Zhang Q, Li C, Deligiannis IK, Jiao W, Liu W, Martinez-Jimenez CP, Wang Z, Dyar KA, Zhang L, Sun P. Decoding Cell-level Circadian Phase and Rhythm-state Remodeling in Single-cell Transcriptomes With scRhythmBayes. bioRxiv v1, posted October 22, 2026. DOI: 10.64898/2026.10.18.759931.
**Source:** https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.18.759931v1
**Substantive abstract retrieved:** https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.18.759931v1.full
**Evidence depth:** Abstract-level; PDF retrieval failed. No numerical superiority, complete split audit or neural validation claimed.
**Research problem:** Sample collection time is not the exact circadian phase of every cell, and phase heterogeneity can obscure cell-type/condition-dependent rhythmic changes.
**Method:** Seed-gene count likelihood plus stratum-aware circular prior to infer cellular phase, propagate phase support into gene-level models, and estimate posterior probabilities of rhythm gain/loss, amplitude remodeling and phase shifts. [Abstract.]
**Key result:** Authors report improved phase recovery in heterogeneous simulations, temporal zonation differences in mouse hepatocytes, and cell-type-selective temporal disorganization in human heart disease. Stress tests identify dependence on seed genes, time anchoring, sampling density and cell number. [Abstract.]
**What is new here:** Connects uncertain cell-level phase with gene-level rhythm-state remodeling instead of treating clock time or average expression as complete cellular state.
**Why it matters for AIVC:** Suggests a missing-state/nuisance question for identity-plus-environment models: apparent neighborhood or disease effects might partially reflect temporal programs. This is a hypothesis, not a finding about SCZ/DLPFC or the user's data.
**What is weak / unresolved:** Depends on adequate seed/time support; liver/heart evidence does not establish recoverable circadian phase in postmortem cortical nuclei. Inferred phase can absorb genuine disease biology if indiscriminately regressed out. No perturbation prediction or causal temporal regulation is demonstrated by the inspected abstract.
**Future research idea:** Where collection-time and sample-quality metadata support it, test whether neighborhood gains persist after a pre-specified, uncertainty-aware circadian sensitivity analysis. Fit phase inference within training folds, preserve disease contrasts, and explicitly allow phase to be unidentifiable rather than force a correction.
**Decision:** SAVE as a potential confounder/control analysis, not an immediate required preprocessing step.
